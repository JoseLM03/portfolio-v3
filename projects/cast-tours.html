01 / THE PROBLEM

Travelers need exact facts. Hallucinations break itineraries.

In guided tour operations, a fabricated meeting time, wrong baggage allowance, or invented refund rule creates real disruption.

CAST Tours operates specialized international concert and culture tours across multiple destinations. Each trip carries unique inclusions, concert ticket policies, luggage limits, and schedules. When travelers ask questions, standard generative chatbots introduce two unacceptable risks:

First, an unconstrained model can easily blend details across different trips—answering a question about one itinerary with policies belonging to another. Second, travelers often paste personal booking numbers, passenger names, or payment inquiries into chat inputs, which must never be forwarded to third-party AI endpoints.

Furthermore, CAST Tours serves a diverse bilingual traveler base in English and Spanish across desktop and mobile devices. The goal was to build an assistant that behaves like an experienced travel coordinator: answers must be grounded strictly in approved documentation, customer data must be screened deterministically before external calls, and whenever an answer cannot be verified with certainty, the system must hand off cleanly to human staff.

03 / HOW IT WORKS

Deterministic boundaries. Scoped retrieval. Grounded answers.

The architecture ensures external AI models never make policy decisions or access unscreened input. Deterministic Python services govern privacy screening, trip scoping, evidence retrieval, and output validation.

04 / ENGINEERING

Decisions behind the system.

01

Preflight privacy screening before external calls.

Customer questions pass through server-side regex screening before external AI generation endpoints are invoked. Booking numbers, payment terms, phone numbers, and email patterns trigger safe routing or sanitization, mitigating the risk of sensitive traveler details being transmitted to third-party APIs.

02

Trip-specific scoping eliminates cross-talk.

Travel inquiries are mapped to a single tour itinerary. The resolver matches questions exclusively to curated knowledge units for the active trip. Inquiries regarding retired or historical tour offerings are intercepted and referred to staff rather than generating speculative answers.

03

Bilingual lexical retrieval & trip-scoped indexing.

In public production, retrieved evidence is drawn directly from approved KnowledgeUnit documentation using deterministic lexical search. By indexing trip itineraries into discrete, verified units with bilingual Spanish and English keyword mapping, the system retrieves relevant clauses without introducing vector drift or semantic mismatch from third-party embedding models. Earlier offline development evaluated semantic embeddings for synthetic benchmarking, while the live release prioritizes verified lexical retrieval for predictable, auditable itinerary matching.

04

Groq low-latency inference with strict JSON schema.

Production inference utilizes Groq acceleration to deliver sub-second response times without sacrificing deterministic schema conformance. Generation runs at temperature 0 constrained by strict Pydantic JSON schemas, ensuring that model outputs map claim strings directly to validated evidence IDs for server-side verification.

05

Deterministic claim validation and staff escalation.

The backend runs automated rule-based checks on cited source IDs, scans for qualified wording, and deterministically escalates to human coordinators whenever an answer cannot be grounded in approved knowledge units—providing a structured safeguard rather than relying on model self-policing.

06

WordPress iframe sandbox & form submission fix (Oct 31).

During the October 31 production deployment onto the client's live WordPress website, the floating widget was embedded inside an isolated iframe with a browser sandbox policy. While allow-scripts and allow-same-origin were enabled, the initial policy omitted allow-forms. Because message dispatch and Enter key submission in the widget rely on form submission semantics, the omission silently prevented visitors from submitting queries. I diagnosed the sandbox restriction, updated the embed configuration to include allow-forms, authored automated regression tests for embed security headers, and verified seamless production operation.

Deployment scope & limitations

CAST Tours AI Chatbot is publicly deployed in production on the CAST Tours WordPress website to provide travelers with verified itinerary assistance. In accordance with portfolio verification standards, this case study does not make unsupported claims regarding traffic volume, sales conversions, uptime percentages, or revenue impact. Itinerary answers reflect curated, approved tour documents rather than unvetted live web searches, and any ungrounded query escalates deterministically to human coordinators.

06 / TAKEAWAY

Building for real travelers taught me defensive engineering.

Working directly with organization stakeholders and delivering a publicly deployed production tool showed me that knowing when not to answer is the most critical feature in practical enterprise AI.

Collaborating with leadership and staff at CAST Tours underscored that operational stakeholders care far more about reliability and predictability than model novelty. An answer that is 95% plausible but hallucinates a bus transfer time, ticket inclusion, or baggage surcharge destroys traveler trust immediately.

Taking the system from initial development to public production on WordPress required engineering rigor at every layer: from deterministic PII preflight screening and Groq low-latency inference to resolving real-world browser sandbox edge cases like the October 31 allow-forms fix. To support systematic evaluation, I built diagnostic timing middleware (cast_tours.timing) that records sub-millisecond durations across preflight filtering, lexical scoring, generation completion, and claim validation. This work reinforced how small, robust software foundations—clear interface contracts, strict schemas, automated regression tests, and defensive fallbacks—turn generative AI into a dependable business tool.
