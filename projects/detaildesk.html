01 / THE PROBLEM

Detailers lose revenue when they can't answer the phone.

Independent auto detailers spend their workdays polishing paint and steam-cleaning interiors—meaning missed phone calls turn directly into lost customers.

Mobile detailers and solo shop owners are frequently unable to pick up phone calls or reply to repetitive text messages regarding pricing packages, service radiuses, and availability while actively working on vehicles. Potential customers often move on to competitors after an unanswered call.

Furthermore, when customers do reach out via text or social media, requests are frequently vague ("how much to clean my SUV?"). Without knowing vehicle condition (pet hair, deep carpet stains, exterior oxidation), detailers cannot accurately estimate time or cost, leading to awkward surprises upon arrival.

DetailDesk tackles this challenge with a two-part solution: an automated, business-grounded virtual receptionist that answers routine questions 24/7 without making up prices, paired with a structured vehicle quote intake engine that captures condition ratings and vehicle photos directly into an owner management dashboard.

03 / ARCHITECTURE

Decoupled full-stack architecture with zero-dependency SQLite.

The system pairs a lightweight React client with an Express API backend, using Node's native SQLite module for frictionless local persistence without complex database setup.

04 / FEATURES

What works in the prototype today.

01

Structured Quote & Appointment Engine

Customers enter contact details, vehicle specifications (year, make, model), preferred appointment date with local timezone validation, a 4-point condition rating (pet hair, stains, odor, paint condition), and an optional vehicle photo upload with instant preview and removal.

02

Rule-Based Virtual Receptionist

Provides 24/7 automated responses regarding detailing packages, starting prices, service radius, and common FAQs strictly based on SQLite business records. It enforces strict guardrails by refusing to invent custom discounts or quotes, directing customers to the quote form.

03

Owner Lead Management Dashboard

Detailing shop owners can view incoming leads in real time, transition statuses (New → Contacted → Booked → Closed), inspect vehicle photos, search customer records, and filter by status or service package.

04

Live Business Profile & Pricing Sync

An owner settings editor allows updating business hours, service radius, packages, starting prices, and approved FAQs. Updates instantly synchronize with the receptionist's knowledge base without server restarts.

What this does not represent

DetailDesk is an active academic prototype, not a deployed commercial SaaS platform. It has no paying customers, no active payment gateway, and no multi-tenant authentication. Vehicle photos are stored in local server storage rather than cloud object storage. The virtual receptionist currently uses deterministic keyword matching rather than an external LLM API.

06 / ROADMAP

Active status and planned next steps.

The prototype proves the core customer-to-owner workflow. Future development will focus on production integrations and cloud infrastructure.

01

Modular LLM Integration

Upgrade the virtual receptionist from rule-based matching to a generative model (Gemini API or OpenAI) bounded by strict grounding prompts and system instructions to handle varied natural language questions.

02

Two-Way SMS Notifications

Integrate Twilio to automatically send text confirmations to customers when a quote is submitted and alert owners of new leads immediately while detailing vehicles.

03

Deposit & Payment Processing

Incorporate Stripe or Square to collect non-refundable booking deposits, reducing no-shows and securing scheduled detailing slots.

04

Cloud Storage & Calendar Sync

Migrate vehicle photo uploads from local server storage to cloud object storage (AWS S3 / Google Cloud Storage) and sync appointments with Google Calendar.

07 / TAKEAWAY

What I learned collaborating on a full-stack product.

Building in a team environment highlighted the importance of clear branch hygiene, communicative PR reviews, and defensive frontend validation.

Working alongside Quincy and Dominique taught me how collaborative engineering differs from solo development. Agreeing on interface contracts between the React client and Express endpoints early prevented merge conflicts and duplicate work.

Solving specific bugs—like tracking down UTC timezone shifts in date inputs and managing unmounted DOM file inputs with React refs—reinforced the value of disciplined testing. Seeing teammate contributions merge smoothly into a unified prototype made this one of my most rewarding college software projects.
